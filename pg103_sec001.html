Strong army: The leaders of Benin built a powerful army in the mid-15th century. The army defended the state against external threats. It ensured peace, security, and stability within the state. It also facilitated the expansion of the territory by conquering weaker neighbouring states.

Involvement in trade: The oba of Benin and some of the people in the empire participated in trade with the Portuguese. They took trade items such as ivory, gums, pepper and cotton cloth from Benin to the coast. They sold those goods to Portuguese traders. In addition, Benin artists made saltcellars, ivory carvings, and bangles, which were sold to the Europeans.

Decline of the Benin Kingdom

The empire declined in the 18th century due to a number of factors. The following are some of them:

Civil wars and dynastic disputes: The kingdom of Benin was later faced with civil wars and dynastic disputes. These disputes were sometimes caused by conflicts over succession. The disputes finally resulted in wars, which weakened the state. Internal disputes were, therefore, an important factor for the decline of the Benin Kingdom.

Slave trade: The obas sold their people and war captives to the Portuguese as slaves. Despite the fact that the slave trade was one of the sources of Benin’s wealth, it had negative effects on the production capacity of the empire. This was because slave trade removed productive people from their respective areas. This caused shortage of labour. In the end, this led to the decline of the state.

British invasion: British troops invaded Benin in 1897 and robbed brass, bronze, and copper sculptures, which were symbols of power in the kingdom. The invasion also resulted in the signing of several treaties with the people of the Niger Delta. These treaties declared the whole region of the Niger Delta as a British protectorate. With this declaration, Benin lost its independence and autonomy. This marked its decline.

Oyo Kingdom

The Oyo Kingdom was established in the 14th century. Like other Forest states, it originated from the Ife Kingdom. The founders of the Oyo Kingdom are believed to have been descendants of Oduduwa, the founder of the Yoruba states. Unlike other Forest states in Yorubaland, which were situated to the south, Oyo was located north of the tropical forests in the Savannah. It occupied the southwestern corner of the present-day Nigeria. The title of the ruler of the Oyo state was Alaafin.
