Moreover, this kind of education aimed at promoting peace and security in the society. The education trained the youth to defend their societies against enemies. For instance, in the Maasai society the Morans were responsible for peacekeeping. They were also taught how to defend their community, protect their livestock from thieves and wild animals, and raid their neighbouring communities to expand their herds of cattle.

Furthermore, it aimed at preparing the youth to take over after the end of the existing phase of leadership in their societies. This duty was important because administrative knowledge was at the very heart of social, political and economic development. Well-prepared young leaders could enhance the stability of society across generations.

Additionally, pre-colonial education aimed at making the youth patriotic. It was the duty of elders to impart a sense of patriotism to the youth so that they might be proud of their people, environment and resources. In pre-colonial societies, there were different conflicts among states. Therefore, a sense of patriotism was very important among the youth to discourage conspiracy against the welfare of their societies.

Characteristics of pre-colonial education

Pre-colonial education was practical in nature. For example, the youth were taken to the seas, oceans, rivers and lakes, where they were taught how to fish. A similar practice was done among the agricultural communities, who taught young people how to farm. Moreover, those interested in iron smithery or industries were practically taught how to make iron or other industrial goods.

Pre-colonial education lacked uniformity. It varied from one society to another, depending on a given society’s economic, social and environmental conditions. Each society had its customs and traditions. For example, the customs and traditions of the societies that lived in the arid and semi-arid environments differed from those found around rivers and lakes.
