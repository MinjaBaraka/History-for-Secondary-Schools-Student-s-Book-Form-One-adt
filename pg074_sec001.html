Uses of salt

Salt had many uses in the pre-colonial period. It served as a food ingredient and preservative. Foodstuff, such as beef and fish lasted longer when salted. Salt was also used as a medium of exchange because it was a rare commodity in many societies. Therefore, it became an important commodity in trade. For example, salt from Uvinza was a commodity of exchange all over the Great Lakes region. In addition, the people of Ethiopia and the merchants of Timbuktu in the Sahara Desert used salt as a medium of exchange. Likewise, salt was used for the treatment of wounds. In some societies, people used salt as medicine for curing wounds. Sodium bicarbonate was used as antacid to treat indigestion and upset stomach as it reduces stomach acid. Salt was also used for preserving dead bodies from decaying. This was common among ancient Egyptians. This practice of body preservation using salt is called mummification.
