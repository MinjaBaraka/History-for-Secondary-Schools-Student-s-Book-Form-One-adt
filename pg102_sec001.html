Collection of revenues: Ife rulers managed to collect taxes from traders and tributes from peasants. The people of Ife had trade links with the Sahel people who were famous for copper and salt mining. The Kingdom collected tax from copper and salt traders from the Sahel. In addition, it collected tributes from its peasants who exchanged their food and kola nuts with copper and salt from the Sahel region.

Good leadership: Good leadership of Oduduwa was another important factor for the rise and growth of this state. He managed to unite his people and formed a strong and well-organized state. In addition, Oduduwa was highly respected by his subjects partly because he was regarded as the son of Olorun.

Fall of the Ife Kingdom

Ife Kingdom declined following the growing influence of the Oyo Kingdom in the Yorubaland. The Oyo Kingdom invaded Ibadan, an important town in Yorubaland, which was by then controlled by Ife. After the invasion, Ife lost control of Ibadan and became weak. Later, Oyo invaded Ife and settled in its territory. This marked the total collapse of Ife.

The Benin Kingdom

Benin was found in the southern part of Ife near the Niger Delta. It was located deep into the forest. The area occupied by the Kingdom is modern southwestern Nigeria. Therefore, the present-day country of Benin has no connection with the ancient Benin Kingdom. The state was developed by Edo-speaking people in the 13th century. The tittle of the King of Benin was Oba. The kings (oba) of Benin claimed to have descended from Oduduwa, the founding ancestor of the Yoruba. The first Oba of Benin was Oranmiyan, the son of Oduduwa, who is said to have been brought to Benin by the early inhabitants in order to solve conflicts among the chiefs. By the 15th century, Benin had grown into a huge centralised state enclosed with a wall. When Europeans came to Benin, they described it as the Great City of Benin for its size and decorative buildings.

Factors for the rise of the Benin Kingdom

Factors for the rise of Benin Kingdom were similar to those of other states. The only difference was the context. The following thus, were the factors for the rise of the Benin Kingdom;

Strong leadership: The Kingdom had some strong leaders such as Ewuare, also known as Ewuare the Great. He was responsible for turning the city-state of Benin into a kingdom. Under his leadership, the kingdom was well organised and strengthened. Oba Ewuare appointed his royal district chiefs who ensured the stability of the empire. He established a good oba succession system, whereby his oldest son was recognized officially as the successor of the kingdom.
