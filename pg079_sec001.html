Patrilineal social system

This is the most common form of kinship practised worldwide, including Africa. People who practise it trace their kin through their fathers. When a woman is married, she moves to her husband’s clan and becomes part of his kin group. Children in this social system take and follow the names of their father’s clan. Examples of societies that followed the patrilineal system included the Luo of Kenya and Tanzania, the Zulu of South Africa, the Ndebele of Zimbabwe, and the Ngoni and Tumbala of Northern Zambia. One of the characteristics of the patrilineal kinship was that men on the father’s line dominated social, economic and political decisions. They made important decisions concerning the welfare and interests of the family or clan. They also owned and determined the use of the major means of production, like land. They decided on the amount and distribution of the bridewealth and oversaw marriage transactions.

Matrilineal social system

Under the matrilineal social system, a married man moves from his father’s family and clan to live with his wife’s family. He may live within the wife’s family compound or nearby. Children take and follow the names of their mother’s clan. Thus, clan inheritance is based on the mother’s clan. In pre-colonial Africa, fathers had no power over children in matrilineal societies. Maternal uncles made all important decisions concerning their nephews and nieces, including deciding on bride wealth. Some societies that followed the matrilineal system were the Luguru of Morogoro, the Yao and the Makonde of Lindi and Mtwara, the Bemba of Zambia, the Mbundu of Kingdom of Ndongo in Angola, the Herero of Namibia and Botswana as well as the Ashanti and Akan of Ghana.

Characteristics of clan or kinship organisation

The following were the characteristics of the clan or kinship organisation.

A clan or kinship had common rituals, taboos, and customs. These cultural characteristics helped create stability, strength, and uniqueness of each clan from other clans. They helped to bind members of the clan together and forge a sense of community.
