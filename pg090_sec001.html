Bunyoro Kingdom

The Bunyoro Kingdom is traced back to the kingdom of Bunyoro-Kitara founded by the Bachwezi people in the 13th century. By the 16th century, Bunyoro-Kitara had started to decline and gave rise to new and smaller kingdoms. One of the new kingdoms, which emerged, was Bunyoro. The title of the king of Bunyoro was Omukama.

Factors for the rise of Bunyoro Kingdom

There were specific factors for the rise of the Bunyoro Kingdom. They included the following:

Influence of traditions: Traditions had great influence on choosing the Omukama. The Omukama was chosen from among the chiefs who came from the ruling family or the royal dynasty of Babito. This practice helped reduce disputes over power succession among the provincial chiefs who were called Saza.

Role of strong kings: The Bunyoro Kingdom needed strong leadership in order to maintain its position. Such leadership was provided by famous kings like Omukama Kabarega who helped Bunyoro to grow into a powerful kingdom in the 19th Century. Kabalega Kamurasi created the Bunyoro’s standing army, which consisted of about 20,000 soldiers. His soldiers were called Abarusura. He divided his army into ten divisions, each under a commander. These were then deployed in the provinces to defend the empire against external invasions. They also checked the internal revolts against the central government. Apart from ensuring law and order, the army was also used to raid neighbouring states of Toro, Ankole, and Busoga during the wars of conquest and expansion.

Influence of the geographical conditions: Bunyoro was one of the most fertile areas in the Great Lakes region. Its climate was good, and enabled the people of Bunyoro to grow various crops and raise livestock. They produced enough food, which supported their society including rulers and soldiers.

Strong and efficient political system: The kingdom was divided into provinces. These provinces were ruled by provincial chiefs known as Saza who were appointed by the Omukama. The Saza controlled land on behalf of the Omukama. These chiefs were loyal to the king. In their provinces, they distributed land to peasants and cattle herders in return for loyalty, services, and tributes. They helped to promote peace and unity among the people of Bunyoro.
