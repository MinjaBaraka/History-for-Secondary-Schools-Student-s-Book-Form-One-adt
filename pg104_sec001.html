Factors for the rise of Oyo Kingdom

There were several factors that contributed to the rise of Oyo Kingdom. The following were some factors:

Invasion by the Bariba and Nupe: The Oyo Kingdom rose because of the invasion of the Ife Kingdom by the Bariba and Nupe. This invasion caused the people to move from Ife Kingdom to Oyo Ile (Old Oyo). In Oyo Ile, they established outlying towns that formed the new Oyo Kingdom.

Military strength: The rulers of Oyo built a centralised state using a standing army. In addition, the Kingdom was located in a tsetse fly-free zone, which made the use of horses possible. During this period, horses were important for the efficiency of the army, since soldiers used them during warfare.

Involvement in trade: The Kingdom participated in the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade and in other trade interactions with its neighbours. It gained access to the trade after invading the coastal kingdom of Allada. The Kingdom was one of the sole protectors of the trade between the coast and Hausa land, which benefited the Kingdom largely. Through this trade, Oyo rulers managed to collect a lot of tributes and taxes from villages and towns as well as from transit traders. These revenues benefited rulers and the state in general.

Strong and organised leadership: The kingdom had strong leaders like Alaafin Orompoto and Abiodun. They managed to organise the state well and stabilise its economy. These leaders were chosen by the council of seven chiefs (Oyo Mesi) from the lineage that traced its origin to Oduduwas or Oduduwas’ son, Oronyan. Oyo Mesi was headed by the Basorun. The system of checks and balance also ensured the presence of good leaders. Oyo Mesi also had the ability to force the Alaafin to renounce the throne and commit suicide when he did not perform well.

Fall of Oyo Kingdom

The Oyo Kingdom collapsed in the 19th century. The following were some of the reasons for its decline.

Leadership problems: The decline of the Oyo Kingdom started when some of its leaders failed to rule the state effectively. Some of them lacked wisdom and governed contrary to the established norms of the kingdom. One such leader was Aole (also known as Awole) who took the throne in 1789. Aole ordered the attack of Apomu, one of the towns of Ife, against the oath he had taken.
