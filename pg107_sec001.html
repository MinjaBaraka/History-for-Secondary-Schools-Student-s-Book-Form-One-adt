Kongo Kingdom

The Kongo Kingdom was one of the most important kingdoms in Central Africa. It started towards the end of the 14th century. By 1482, when the Portuguese arrived, the kingdom was highly centralised. The title of the king was Mani Kongo. The capital of the kingdom was called Mbanza. The Portuguese later called the capital San Salvador. The first Mani Kongo of the kingdom was Nzinga Nkuwu.

Factors for the rise of the Kongo Kingdom

The Kongo Kingdom rose to power due to various factors. The following are some of them:

Development of arts and crafts: The Bakongo were highly developed in arts and crafts. They were skilled metal workers, potters, and weavers. Fine clothes woven from the fibre of raffia palm were widely traded along the Atlantic coast. They were exchanged for salt and seashells, which were used as local currency.

Frequent movement of the people: People frequently moved in and out of the Kongo Kingdom. This led to the emergence of new villages with different clans. Those clans needed a political organisation to unite them. The Kongo state provided strong centralised leadership to those clans.

Religious beliefs: The power and authority of the king partly depended on religious beliefs. The Mani Kongo was considered a semi-god by his people. He used spiritual powers to unite the people in his kingdom. These powers made all provinces to pay tribute to the king. The tributes helped him strengthen the central government.

Monopoly of trade: The king of Kongo monopolised trade in the kingdom, including the slave trade. He introduced taxes to traders passing through the kingdom. He also collected tributes from vassal states. The taxes and tributes were used for strengthening and consolidating the kingdom.

Favourable climatic conditions: The Kongo Kingdom was located in a heavy forest region. The area had sufficient rainfall and fertile soil that supported the development of agriculture. Agriculture, in turn, provided sufficient food to feed the growing population in the Kingdom.

Population increase: As people increased in number, it became necessary for them to organise themselves under a strong political authority. This is because there was a need to ensure law and order in the society and security against external threats.
