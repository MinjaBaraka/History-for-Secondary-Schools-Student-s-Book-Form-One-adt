Factors for the weakening of Buganda Kingdom

Despite all the factors that led to the rise of Baganda Kingdom, it finally became weak. The following were the factors for the weakening of the Buganda Kingdom.

Establishment of colonialism: The establishment of the colonial rule undermined political power and authority of Kabaka. The Buganda Agreement of 1900 allowed the Governor to control Buganda. Under this agreement, Kabaka Mwanga unwillingly submitted to British control and became part of the British protectorate. As part of the British protectorate, the Kingdom was no longer free. This greatly reduced the power of Kabaka, and led to the weakening of the Kingdom.

Religious differences and conflicts: There were three conflicting religious groups, namely Christianity, Islam, and the indigenous religion. These conflicts occurred during the second half of the 19th century. These groups divided the Buganda Kingdom into conflicting religious ideologies. Buganda gradually lost its internal unity and stability as a socio-political entity, and led to its weakening.

Introduction of colonial economy: The introduction of a peasant economy by British colonial officials changed the production system in Buganda and brought in the disintegration of the kingdom. People started to produce cash crops, which reduced the production of food crops. As a result, the food available could not sufficiently feed the entire population in the kingdom.

Conflicting administrative interests: Since many Batongole ruled Buganda, some provincial chiefs tried to gain absolute power and authority over their subjects. These efforts resulted in administrative misunderstandings and conflicts between Batongole and Kabaka. The situation brought the whole kingdom into administrative problems that finally led to its weakening.
