Advantages of using iron tools

The use of iron tools and weapons had many advantages. It led to the improvement of tools, which in turn facilitated the expansion of agriculture and increased food production. Iron tools also simplified the mining of iron and non-iron minerals and the making of other handcrafts. Moreover, communities were able to increase production of different items. This led to the rise and development of trade. In some African areas, people used iron bars as currency. Iron technology consolidated sedentary or settled life. This was because the technology enabled people to establish homes and use iron weapons to defend their families and properties against enemies. It also contributed to the rise of states in some parts of Africa, such as Fipaland, Buganda, and Karagwe. Similarly, the use of iron weapons made great changes in warfare. Societies with iron weapons became stronger. They conquered weaker ones, which were still using stone and wooden weapons. These conquests led to the expansion of chiefdoms or kingdoms. It also increased the frequency of wars among rival communities.

Salt making

Salt making was among the earliest industries in pre-colonial Africa. There were four main methods of obtaining and processing salt. These were salt extraction from salty reeds, leaching of rocky soils, salt mining from rocks, and evaporating salty water.

Salt extraction from salty reeds:

Salty reeds were burnt and the ash was dissolved in water. The solution was filtered and boiled until all the water evaporated. The remaining residue was used as salt. This method was common among the Mang’anja people along Lake Nyasa, also around Lake Victoria and Lake Albert in East Africa. It was also commonly practised among the people of the interlacustrine region.

Leaching:

Leaching is a method by which water was drained through rocky soils that had a high content of salt. It was sieved and boiled to obtain salt crystals. The method was practised at Uvinza and Ivuna in Tanzania and at Bilma in West Africa.

Salt mining from rocks:

In this method, people directly mined salty rocks. Then, the rock crystals were used as salt. This method was used by people around Bangwelo and Luapula rivers in Central Africa and Lake Chad in Western Sudan. In West Africa, the method was used at Bilma, Taghaza, Idjil, and Taoudeni.
